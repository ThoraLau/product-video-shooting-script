# VOC and Positioning

Read this reference when the user supplies reviews, interviews, comments, survey responses, support logs, or competitor comparisons.

## Extract lived consumer truth

For each meaningful statement, identify the situation, friction, workaround, desired outcome, proof language, and limitation.

Translate technical features into this chain:

`feature → physical effect → use-context benefit → emotional or practical payoff`

Example:

`detachable shoulder strap → bag leaves the bicycle → no repacking after locking up → errands continue without interruption`

## Use positive and negative evidence

Positive reviews reveal valued outcomes and natural phrasing. Negative reviews prevent overclaiming and often sharpen positioning.

- If users say a bag lacks rigid support, position it for clothing and ordinary daily cargo rather than cameras or unsupported electronics.
- If a component receives durability complaints, do not make durability the campaign claim without stronger evidence.
- If customers praise easy removal more than capacity, make the transition experience the lead idea and capacity supporting evidence.
- If customers disagree on size, use concrete packing proof rather than “huge” or “fits everything.”

Do not copy reviews as testimonials unless the user asks and usage rights are clear. Use them to understand the buyer's life and decision logic.

## Positioning test

A useful positioning sentence identifies the specific user, recurring situation, job the product performs, alternative or friction removed, and credible reason to believe.

Avoid empty claims such as “premium,” “ultimate,” “game-changing,” or “no weaknesses.” Replace them with an end-to-end experience that is visibly better.
