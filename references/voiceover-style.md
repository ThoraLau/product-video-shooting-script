# Voiceover Style

## Write Chinese before English

First write a Chinese mother script that shows complete product understanding and real consumer life. It may be more expressive than the final English, but every sentence must map to a shot or proof.

Then adapt the meaning into natural spoken English. Do not translate Chinese syntax word for word.

## Beginner and everyday-user voice

- Speak from a recognizable moment, not from a specification sheet.
- Use ordinary situations: commuting, locking up, coffee runs, shopping, sudden rain, blocked views, tired shoulders, or forgotten tools.
- Explain why a mechanism matters without assuming technical knowledge.
- Sound observant and confident, not like a product manual or overexcited influencer.

## Wiggle-inspired rhythm

- Short, conversational, action-driven sentences.
- Prefer executable verbs: lock, lift, loop, twist, press, release, pack, grab, extend, clip, ride.
- Keep cause and benefit in the same beat when possible.
- Use numbers only when they create clarity or proof.
- Open with a pain, question, surprising distinction, or visible result.
- End with a short question plus suggestion when suitable.

Useful patterns:

- `Problem? Action—result.`
- `Need more [benefit]? Do [visible action].`
- `Still [old friction]? Try [product behavior].`
- `Ride with it. Walk with it.`

## Synchronization

At ordinary advertising pace, budget roughly 2.3–2.8 English words per second, then adjust for pauses, product names, numbers, and physical actions. Put secondary specifications on screen if the voice becomes crowded.

Each line must match what is visible. If narration says “stays dry inside,” open the bag after the rain test and reveal a dry item. If it says “no sway,” show a loaded shake or rough-road test.

## Logic checks

- Attribute clarity to the optical element and stability to the mount; do not merge unrelated causes.
- Attribute compatibility only to verified dimensions or supported fit language.
- Use “rain-ready,” “water-resistant,” or “waterproof” only at the evidence level supported by specifications and the filmed test.
- Do not turn “fits most” into “fits every.”
