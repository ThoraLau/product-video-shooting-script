# Shooting Script Standard

## Required columns

| Column | Required content |
|---|---|
| 时间 | Shot start/end or duration |
| 希望消费者理解的信息 | One intended belief, benefit, or resolved objection |
| 画面与动作 | Subject position, product state, hand/model action, and visible result |
| 镜头与光线 | Only the shot size, angle, movement, focus, and lighting needed to create the proof |
| 英文口播／屏幕文字 | Exact final English narration and exact on-screen copy |
| 场地与道具 | Only essential locations, products, test materials, and props |

## Design shots by purpose

Before retaining a shot, answer:

1. What should the consumer understand after this shot?
2. What visible evidence makes that conclusion credible?

Remove or redesign any shot that cannot answer both.

Choose the camera treatment that reveals the claim:

- Side profile for thickness, shape, clearance, or installed relationship.
- Top-down for packing, dimensions, layout, and step sequences.
- Macro for controls, seams, locking points, labels, texture, or material behavior.
- Matched comparison for before/after, stock/product, size, or deformation.
- Low rear or side tracking for stability, load, wheel clearance, and real riding.
- Controlled back/side light for droplets, surface finish, texture, and silhouette.

Camera variation must reveal new information. Do not change angles merely to make the edit look busy.

## Default persuasive progression

Use the smallest sequence that proves the purchase case:

1. pain, tension, or product distinction;
2. immediate product identification;
3. main action or mechanism;
4. detail, measurement, capacity, or installation proof;
5. real-use or stress-test result;
6. destination payoff and CTA.

Reorder when the strongest proof deserves to open the video.

## Production restraint

These scripts primarily support product-display work. Keep acting minimal and functional. A model may carry, install, press, ride, lock, retrieve, or enter a location; avoid fictional story beats and emotional performance directions unless requested.

Include reference images only when they materially remove ambiguity about layout, angle, lighting, or action. Do not overload the final table with exhaustive equipment notes.
